The Neues Reha-Zentrum Böllstein occupies a steeply inclined site on the edge of the village of Böllstein in Brombachtal, Hessen — between the Etbacher Straße at ±0.00 m and the Kirchbrombacher Straße nine metres below. Rather than levelling the hillside, the design exploits its topography as the primary ordering device, distributing four distinct programmes across four vertical levels, each with its own relationship to ground and sky.

At the deepest level (−10.00 m) lies the investigation wing: a quiet, earth-embedded floor housing radiology, MRT, blood sampling, and consultation rooms for an independent orthopaedic practice. Above it (−5.00 m), the movement and thermal programme unfolds — gymnasium, physiotherapy, sauna, cold bath, hydrotherapy pool, and staff accommodation. Barefoot pathways connect treatment rooms to a green outdoor zone carved into the slope.

The entry level (±0.00 m) is the social heart of the centre: the main entrance bridges the two streets, drawing visitors through a café, creative workshop, meditation and prayer room, communal kitchen, and into a quiet atrium open to the forest beyond. Forested hiking routes begin directly from the building's south edge.

The upper residential floor (+4.00 m) provides 25 m² barrier-free assisted living units, each with its own terrace, clustered around a library and a theatre staircase that descends through the building section.
